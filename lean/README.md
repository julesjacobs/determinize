Review these three entry points and the definitions they import:

- `Determinize/Statement/Main.lean` defines the expectation-preservation propositions directly: `mainThm` (finite expectations), `extendedExpectationThm` (expectations in the extended reals, infinite values included), `jensenThm` (Jensen's inequality between the two output laws), `outputMassThm` (equal output mass), `varianceThm` (non-increasing second moment and variance) and `conditionalExpectationThm` (equal expectations conditioned on acceptance, the statement behind `observe`). `Statement` contains ordinary syntax, typing, primitive distributions and means, determinization, and semantics.
- `Determinize/Traces/Main.lean` defines trace erasure (`correspondenceThm`), trace soundness (`soundnessThm`) and the law of total variance along traces (`Traces.varianceThm`). `Traces/Semantics.lean` defines the operational traces, the joint law `traceAndOutputLaw` of a program's trace and output, and `outputGivenTrace`, the program replayed along a trace.
- `Determinize/Theorems.lean` proves all nine propositions without additional hypotheses and prints their axioms.

Run `lake build --wfail` from this directory; the build is warning-free and contains no `sorry`. Check that all nine axiom reports contain only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` (`.claude/scripts/check.sh lean` performs both checks). With Lean's kernel and these standard axioms trusted, reviewers can omit the proof bodies in `Proof`. `Statement` imports no tracing or proof modules; `Traces` imports no proof modules.

Source expressions need not be in ANF. Each primitive distribution is its own constructor with the paper's operands (`uniform mode kind lower upper`, `gaussian mode kind mean variance`, and so on); a site carries its mode and whether it still samples or already returns the primitive's mean. Operands may contain nested sampling and are evaluated left to right; a mean site also evaluates every operand exactly once, including a Gaussian's variance. `Expr.sourceForm` excludes mean sites. `Statement/Primitives.lean` gives each primitive one fiber: its law at a stochastic site, the Dirac mass at its mean at a mean site, and the zero measure outside the parameter domain. Expressions have no type annotations; `Typed` assigns types separately. Expressions carry E/G labels only on sample sites; literals and arithmetic are mode-free and a literal types at either mode, as in the paper's `FloatLit`; promotion is explicit; variables are de Bruijn indices. `Typed` enforces the mode restrictions: E multiplication requires a G left operand, division a G denominator, and comparisons G operands. Arithmetic uses real numbers, with `x / 0 = 0`. The theorems quantify over closed float programs of either mode; the proof promotes a general-mode program internally, and the compact trace law does not see that step.

`Kind` is the target language's half of a site: `Expr.determinize` switches every expectation-mode site from `stochastic` to `mean` (`determinizeKind`), and a mean site is not a literal but a site that still evaluates its operands left to right, checks the primitive's parameter domain and then returns the Dirac mass at the primitive's mean (`Statement/Primitives.lean`). General-mode draws nested in the operands of a determinized site are therefore still executed and recorded, which is why source and target share one trace law (`Traces.soundnessThm`). `determinizeKind` never produces the pair `(G, mean)`: a general-mode site keeps its kind and source form has stochastic sites only, so that combination is reachable only by writing it by hand.

The discrete primitives are formalized as follows. `bernoulli mode kind probability` is a seventh primitive constructor, typed like `poisson` with its probability at the site's mode; its fiber `bernoulliFiber` is the two-point law with mass `p` at `1` and `1 - p` at `0` (domain `0 ≤ p ≤ 1`) or the Dirac mass at its mean `p`. Hence `bernoulli_E(p)` is determinized to its mean `p` (a mean site) and `bernoulli_G(p)` keeps sampling, exactly like the other primitives, and the theorems cover it. `flip` is sugar rather than a constructor: `Expr.flip p` is `0 < bernoulli_G(p)`, a general-mode Bernoulli draw compared with `0`, so it is `true` with probability `p`. A Boolean has no mean and only feeds control flow, so a `flip` is never determinized (`Expr.determinize_flip`: determinization only descends into the probability), and because comparisons take general-mode operands only, `flip` needs a general-mode probability (`Typed.flip`, a derived rule; `Proof/Examples.lean` checks that `flip(uniform_E(0, 1))` is rejected). `discrete mode kind weights` is an eighth primitive with literal weights only, as in the implementation's parser (`discrete(p₁, …, pₙ)` draws the index `i ∈ {0, …, n - 1}` with probability `pᵢ`); its fiber `discreteFiber` is the categorical law on the indices (domain: nonnegative weights that sum to one) or the Dirac mass at its mean `∑ i, pᵢ · i`, and its name `Op.discrete n` records the number of weights so that every primitive has a fixed number of parameters. Hence `discrete_E(p₁, …, pₙ)` is determinized to its mean `∑ i, pᵢ · i` and `discrete_G` keeps sampling, which is what `ocaml/determinize.ml` does for the parser's index form; the implementation's internal `discrete` over arbitrary case expressions is not formalized.

Observation is formalized as follows. `observe condition` is a constructor typed `Typed context condition .bool → Typed context (.observe condition) .unit`; it evaluates its condition like `promote` evaluates its operand and then takes one of two steps: `observe(true)` continues with `unit` (`reduce (.observe (.bool true)) = .next .unit`) and `observe(false)` rejects the execution (`reduce (.observe (.bool false)) = .reject`, a fourth reduction action next to `next`, `sample` and `stuck`). A rejected execution contributes nothing: `cumulativeOutputMeasure` and the trace law `Traces.traceAndOutputLawAt` send `reject` to the zero measure, so the output laws are unnormalized sub-probability measures whose mass is the probability of terminating with a real and passing every observation on the way. Rejection is not stuckness (`DoesNotGetStuckAt` sends `reject` to `True`), and the validity hypothesis of the theorems allows it. Because every Boolean in the language is general-mode information (`lt` compares general-mode operands and `promote` only goes from `G` to `E`), a condition never depends on an expectation-mode draw, so the same traces are rejected in the source and in the target and trace soundness is unaffected; `Expr.determinize` descends into the condition without changing its value. `conditionalExpectationThm` is what makes `observe` meaningful: under source integrability, `(∫ v ∂target) / (target univ).toReal = (∫ v ∂source) / (source univ).toReal`, that is, the expectations conditioned on acceptance agree, because both the unnormalized mean (`mainThm`) and the acceptance mass (`outputMassThm`) are preserved. Rejection sampling on the determinized program is therefore justified. Internally, `Proof/Internal/Semantics.lean` lets the one-step kernel send a rejection to the value `unit`, a sink that never yields a real output; this keeps the kernel of a program that is not stuck a probability kernel and leaves every output and trace law unchanged.

Output laws are defined directly by recursion over reduction depth. Deterministic actions continue evaluation; sampling actions integrate the continuation over the primitive measure on reals. Expressions of every type may occur during evaluation, but only terminal reals contribute output. Neither evaluator requires a measurable structure on expressions. `Proof` introduces one internally to establish measurability of the evaluators.

A trace is a list of `(primitive, value)` pairs recording only stochastic G draws. Deterministic steps and E draws add no entry. `traceAndOutputLaw program` is the joint law of a terminating trace and the returned real, `traceLaw program` its trace marginal, and `outputGivenTrace program trace` is the program replayed along `trace`: general-mode draws are read from the trace in order (the zero measure when the trace does not fit, that is, a general-mode site finds no entry or an entry of another primitive, or an entry is left over when the program returns), expectation-mode draws are still integrated, and the result is the law of the returned real. Trace soundness (`Traces.soundnessThm`) states that the source's joint law is its trace law followed by `outputGivenTrace source` (`traceThenOutput`), that the target's joint law is the same trace law followed by `outputGivenTrace target`, and that for almost every trace `outputGivenTrace source trace` has a finite mean and `outputGivenTrace target trace` is the Dirac mass at that mean: conditioned on the general-mode draws, the determinized program returns the conditional mean of the source. The target therefore has the same trace law and the same termination probability as the source. No global integrability is required. Detailed traces with one entry per reduction step live in `Proof/Internal/StepTraces.lean`; `Proof/CompactReplay.lean` packages the compact replay as a measurable kernel and bounds its mass, `Proof/CompactFiberSoundness.lean` repeats the symbolic lockstep argument for it, and `Proof/TraceFactorization.lean` holds the proof-internal factorization through a Markov kernel and a measurable output function, from which `Proof/Corollaries.lean` derives every corollary.

The finite expectation theorem adds source integrability and proves target integrability and equal integrals. The extended-real theorem only assumes that one of `∫ v⁺` and `∫ v⁻` under the source output law is finite, and concludes the same for the target and equal extended-real expectations. Jensen's inequality bounds `∫ φ` under the target output law by `∫ φ` under the source output law for every nonnegative convex `φ : ℝ → ℝ`. `outputMassThm` states that the two output laws have the same total mass: both equal the mass of the trace law, because every fiber is a probability measure, so expectations conditioned on termination agree as well. `varianceThm` assumes a finite second moment of the source output law (`MemLp id 2`) and concludes that the target output law has one too and that neither the second moment nor Mathlib's `variance` increases. `Traces.varianceThm` is the law of total variance along traces: for every trace factorization of a source program, the fiber variances are integrable over the trace law and the source output variance is the target output variance plus the integral of the fiber variances, so determinization discards exactly the variance of the fibers, trace by trace. Output measures are unnormalized: divergence and rejection contribute no output mass, and expectations are not conditioned on termination. The variance statements nevertheless hold for Mathlib's `variance` (`∫ (v - ∫ v)²`, with the unnormalized mean) as written, because both output laws have the same mass and the same mean; dividing the output laws and the trace law by that common mass gives the same statements for the laws conditioned on termination and acceptance, and `conditionalExpectationThm` states the resulting equality of conditional expectations directly. All corollaries are derived from trace soundness in `Proof/Corollaries.lean`.

The default build checks nested sampling, the `x + 1/y` example, a general-mode draw scaling an expectation-mode draw from the left, a sampled value captured by a function, an expectation-mode `bernoulli` draw with a literal and with a nested expectation-mode probability together with its determinization to the mean site, a `flip` inside an `ite`, the rejection of a `flip` with an expectation-mode probability, a `discrete` draw whose determinization returns the mean `∑ i, wᵢ · i`, and `let x = uniform_G(0, 1) in let _ = observe(x < 1/2) in promote x + uniform_E(0, 1)` (typed, in source form, its determinization, `DoesNotGetStuck` although half of its executions are rejected, and its target joint law as the trace law followed by `outputGivenTrace`), together with the checks that `observe(false)` rejects and contributes no output or trace mass and that observing an expectation-mode comparison is ill-typed. `Proof/InterfaceChecks.lean` checks the direct evaluator using only public imports and verifies that these imports provide no measurable structure on expressions.

## Deviations from the paper

The statements follow the paper's theorems, not its letter. Reviewers comparing against `tex/` should know:

- **Explicit coercions instead of subsumption.** The paper's `Subsumption` rule and structural subtyping (`Sub-Prod`, `Sub-Sum`, `Sub-List`, `Sub-Arr`) are replaced by the explicit `promote` from `Float[G]` to `Float[E]`, on floats only. A paper-typed program has to be coerced by hand (insert `promote`, map over pairs and lists, eta-expand functions); that elaboration is not formalized, so the formalized language is a fragment of the paper's.
- **Mode labels on sample sites.** The paper's transformation `⟦e : τ⟧` is type-directed; here `Expr.determinize` is a function on terms, so every sample site carries its mode and that label decides whether the site is switched to its mean (`determinizeKind`). Literals and arithmetic carry no labels.
- **Multiplication and division.** `Typed.mul` types `e₁ × e₂` at `Float[m]` whenever `e₁ : Float[G]` and `e₂ : Float[m]`, for arbitrary expressions, so a general-mode scaling factor stands on the left and the expectation-mode factor on the right; `Typed.div` types `e₁ / e₂` at `Float[m]` whenever `e₁ : Float[m]` and `e₂ : Float[G]`. This is the left-G half of the symmetric `[Mul]` rule that `mul-div-typing.md` derives as the mathematically maximal sound one, kept asymmetric so that `Typed` has exactly one rule per constructor and checking against a type is syntax-directed. Since 2026-09-11 the paper's `Mul` (`tex/3_typing.tex`), `ocaml/infer.ml` and `sim/src/compiler/infer.js` use the same rule (in the implementations the right factor is checked against the result type, so a G right factor is admitted by subtyping), replacing the paper's and OCaml's former `Mul-G`/`Mul-ConstL`/`Mul-ConstR` and the sim's former right-G orientation; an E value times a G expression is rejected everywhere and has to be written `y × x`. Division agrees likewise since 2026-09-11: the paper's `Div`, `ocaml/infer.ml` and the sim type `e₁ / e₂` at `Float[m]` when `e₁ : Float[m]` and `e₂ : Float[G]` for arbitrary `e₂`, where the paper and OCaml formerly required a literal denominator (OCaml typed it at the result mode, or accepted `G / G`). `mul-div-typing.md` records the analysis and the decision.
- **Primitive domains.** Sampling outside a primitive's parameter domain (`uniform(a, b)` with `a > b`, a negative Gaussian variance, and so on) yields the zero measure and counts as stuck, and so does a mean site outside the same domain; the paper's mean table is unconditional. `uniform(a, a)` is the Dirac measure at `a`; the paper's table has no such row.
- **Discrete primitives.** `bernoulli` and `discrete` are primitives with the fibers above; `discrete` takes literal weights, as the parser does, and `Op.discrete n` carries the number of weights. Its domain requires the weights to sum to one, which `ocaml/interp.ml` and the sim enforce at the draw (since 2026-09-11; before that OCaml normalized a general-mode draw by the total while `ocaml/determinize.ml` emits the unnormalized `∑ i, pᵢ · i`, so its two sides disagreed for such weights); `Typed.discrete` has no side condition on the weights (an off-domain list is a stuck sample, like every other primitive), while `ocaml/infer.ml` rejects weights outside `[0, 1]` statically. `flip` is the sugar `0 < bernoulli_G(p)`, so its probability must be general mode, as `ocaml/infer.ml` and the sim require since 2026-09-11.
- **Validity hypothesis.** Every theorem assumes `DoesNotGetStuck program`: almost surely, at every reduction depth, no off-domain sample occurs, E draws included. The paper's "valid G-trace" is informal. The hypothesis is necessary: a source that loses mass with positive probability on an off-domain E parameter has a different expectation than its determinization. A failed observation is not stuckness and is allowed by the hypothesis.
- **Observation.** `observe(false)` rejects with zero output mass, which in the unnormalized semantics is equivalent to divergence: both contribute nothing to the output law and to the trace law, and `DoesNotGetStuck` accepts both. The theorems compare the unnormalized laws, whose mass is the probability of terminating and passing every observation, and `conditionalExpectationThm` divides by it. The OCaml instead raises `ObserveFailure` (`ocaml/interp.ml`) and skips the rejected trial, dividing by the number of accepted trials (`ocaml/determinize_main.ml`), which is the normalized reading: the reported average is the expectation conditioned on acceptance, and `conditionalExpectationThm` states that determinization preserves exactly that quantity. The formal `observe` takes a general-mode Boolean; the OCaml `observe : bool -> unit` (`ocaml/infer.ml`) agrees, since Booleans carry no mode there.
- **Expectations.** `Traces.soundnessThm` needs no integrability hypothesis and proves that `outputGivenTrace source trace` is integrable for almost every trace, which the paper assumes as "integrable σ" and states as the open lemma "Mean valuation correctness". `mainThm` covers finite expectations; `extendedExpectationThm` is the paper's extended-real global theorem; `jensenThm` is the paper's global Jensen corollary restricted to real-valued convex functions. `outputMassThm`, `varianceThm`, `Traces.varianceThm` and `conditionalExpectationThm` have no counterpart in the paper; the variance statements use Mathlib's `variance` on the unnormalized output laws, which agrees with the normalized reading because the two laws have the same mass and mean, and `conditionalExpectationThm` normalizes by that mass explicitly.
