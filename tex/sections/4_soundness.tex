\section{Soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

We will now prove our determinization technique sound in the following natural sense: First,
it \emph{preserves the expected return whenever that expectation is well-defined}. Second,
\emph{determinization never increases the variance of returned values}. We proceed by first stating
these results in terms of the output measure $\law e$, and then develop a more fine-grained soundness characterization that also applies when the program's expected return is undefined. We state the main soundness theorems throughout
this section, leaving the proof strategy to~\Cref{sec:symbolic-semantics}.

\subsection{Global Soundness}

% Our results distinguish between finite, infinite, and undefined
% expectations, so let us first make this terminology precise.
A first prerequisite is to agree on the following notions of expectations:

\begin{definition}[Expectation\leanref{expectation-defined}]
Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with probability distribution
$\mu$, and write
%\[
  $r^+=\max(r,0)$,
  and
  $r^-=\max(-r,0)$.
%\]
We define
\[
  \Expect[X^{+}]
  \coloneq \int_{\Real}r^{+}\,\mu(dr)\in[0,\infty]
  \qquad
  \Expect[X^{-}]
  \coloneq \int_{\Real}r^{-}\,\mu(dr)\in[0,\infty]
  \qquad 
  \Expect[X]\coloneqq\Expect[X^+]-\Expect[X^-]~.
\]
%and
%\[
%  \Expect[X]:=\Expect[X^+]-\Expect[X^-].
%\]
The expectation $\Expect[X]$ is \emph{well-defined in the extended reals} if the
two quantities $\Expect[X^{+}]$ and $\Expect[X^{-}]$ are not both infinite. Moreover, $\Expect[X]$ is \emph{finite} iff
$
  \int_{\Real}|r|\,\mu(dr)<\infty
$.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Conditioning on successful return.}
Recall that $\law e$ is a \emph{sub}probability measure since diverging or
rejected executions contribute no returned value. Thus, let
$
  q_e := \law e(\Real)\leanref{return-probability}
$
denote the \emph{probability that $e$ successfully returns}. When $q_e>0$,
we consider expectations and variances conditioned on successful
return. More precisely, we define
\begin{gather*}
  \Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
  :=\frac{1}{q_e}
  \left(
    \int_{\Real}r^+\,\law e(dr)
    -\int_{\Real}r^-\,\law e(dr)
  \right)\quad\leanref{returned-expectation}~,~\text{and}
  \\
  \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
  :=\frac{1}{q_e}
  \int_{\Real}
    \bigl(r-\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]\bigr)^2
  \,\law e(dr)\quad\leanref{returned-variance}~.
\end{gather*}

With these notions at hand, we state our central soundness theorem: Determinization preserves the expected
return whenever the original program's global expectation is well-defined.

\begin{theorem}[Expectation preservation\leanref{expectation-preservation}]
\label{thm:expectation}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. If $q_e>0$ and
$\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]$ is well-defined in the extended reals, then:
%
\begin{enumerate*}
 \item $q_{\trans{e}}>0$,
 \item $\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]$ is well-defined, and
 \item $\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e] =\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]$.
\end{enumerate*}
% then $q_{\trans{e}}>0$ and , and
% \[
%   \Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
%   =\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e].
% \]
\end{theorem}

This theorem requires the original program's
expectation to be well-defined. The distinction is important even for
simple programs, as the following example illustrates.

\begin{example}[Undefined Global Expectation]\label{ex:coin-flip}
Consider a program that flips a fair coin until tails and, after $n$
preceding heads, returns a sample from
$\draw{uniform}\E{-2^n,2^n}$:

\begin{lstlisting}
let sample = rec f bound =>
  if bernoulli[G](1/2) < 1 then uniform[E](-bound, bound)
  else f (2 * bound)
in sample 1
\end{lstlisting}

For any fixed terminating sequence of retained $\G$-samples, the remaining
$\E$-sample has mean zero, so the determinized program returns zero.
Globally, however, let $N$ be the number of heads and $X$ the returned
value. We have $\Pr(N=n)=2^{-(n+1)}$. Conditioned on $N=n$,
$X\sim\kw{uniform}(-2^n,2^n)$ and hence
$\Expect[|X|\mid N=n]=2^{n-1}$.\footnote{First absolute moment. For
$U\sim\kw{uniform}(-a,a)$,
$\Expect[|U|]=(2a)^{-1}\int_{-a}^{a}|u|\,du=a/2$.} Therefore,
\[
  \Expect[|X|]
  ~{}={}~\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Pr(N=n)\Expect[|X|\mid N=n]
  ~{}={}~\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)}2^{n-1}
  ~{}={}~\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac14
  ~{}={}~\infty~.
\]
Because the distribution of $X$ conditioned on $N=n$ is symmetric
around zero for every $n$ and $\Expect[|X|]=\infty$, we have
$\Expect[X^+]=\Expect[X^-]=\infty$. Thus,
$\Expect[X]$ is undefined. Nevertheless, the expected return after
fixing any terminating sequence of retained samples is finite. This observation
motivates a more fine-grained tracewise soundness guarantee that does not require the
global expectation to exist, which we present in the next subsection.
\end{example}

Our second theorem states that determinization never increases the variance of returned values:
%
\begin{theorem}[Variance non-increase\leanref{variance-non-increase}]
\label{thm:variance}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. If $q_e>0$ and
$\int_{\Real}r^2\,\law e(dr)<\infty$, then:
%
\begin{enumerate*}
  \item $q_{\trans e}>0$,
  \item $\trans e$ has a finite second moment, and
  \item $\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e] \leq\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]$.
\end{enumerate*}

% $q_{\trans e}>0$ and $\trans e$ has a finite second moment, and
% \[
%   \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
%   \leq\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e].
% \]
\end{theorem}



\subsection{Tracewise Soundness}
\label{sec:traces}

Although determinization is a syntactic transformation, its semantic
effect can be characterized by conditioning on the random choices that
it retains, namely, the samples annotated with $\G$. Intuitively, once
the outcomes of these $\G$-mode samples are fixed, the remaining
randomness comes only from the $\E$-mode samples that determinization
replaces by their means. Under these fixed $\G$ choices, the
transformed program returns the expected output of the original
program over the remaining $\E$-mode randomness.

This conditional characterization is the key to our soundness result and
is useful for two reasons. First, it gives a local account of what
determinization does even when the program's overall expectation is
undefined. Second, when the global expectation does exist, expectation
preservation follows by integrating over these fixed choices.
Throughout, a property $P(\tau)$ holds $\mu$-almost everywhere if
$\{\tau\mid\neg P(\tau)\}$ is contained in a measurable set of
$\mu$-measure zero.

\input{figures/traces.tex}

We begin by making precise what it means to fix the random choices
retained by determinization.

\begin{definition}[Trace\leanref{traces}]
Let $\mathsf{Prim}$ denote the set of distribution primitives listed in
\cref{fig:semantics}(\subref{fig:semantics-distributions}). We define
the trace space
$\Trace := \bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb N}(\mathsf{Prim}\times\Real)^n$,
equipped with the disjoint-union Borel $\sigma$-algebra.
A \emph{trace} $\tau\in\Trace$ is a finite sequence
\[
  \tau ::= [] \mid (D,r)::\tau,
\]
where each pair $(D,r)$ records the distribution primitive $D$ and
sampled value $r$ of an executed $\G$-mode sampling operation, in
execution order.
\end{definition}

Note that $\E$-mode samples do not appear in a trace: they remain
random after the trace has been fixed. The recorded $\G$-mode samples
in a trace may come from discrete or continuous distributions. The
probability of a trace is the probability that the program successfully
terminates with exactly that sequence of sampling outcomes. We remark that this
probability can be zero, as when the trace specifies the exact outcome
of a sample from a continuous distribution.

Now, we refine the output semantics to jointly record the trace and the
returned value, as shown in \cref{fig:traces}. Like the original output
measure $\law e$, this refined semantics records only executions that
successfully return a real value and is therefore a subprobability
measure; rejected or diverging executions contribute no output pair.
Let $\mathcal D(\Trace\times\mathbb R)$ denote the space of
subprobability measures on $\Trace\times\mathbb R$.
For each $n\in\mathbb N$, the function
$\joint{(\cdot)}n\colon Expr\to\mathcal D(\Trace\times\mathbb R)$
assigns each expression $e$ its joint output measure after \emph{exactly}
$n$ reduction steps. $\E$-mode samples are integrated out, whereas
each $\G$-mode sample contributes its distribution primitive $D$ and
sampled value $r$ to the trace $\tau$. Summing these measures over
all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ gives rise to the function
$J_{(\cdot)}\colon Expr\to\mathcal D(\Trace\times\mathbb R)$,
which assigns each expression $e$ its joint big-step subprobability
measure $J_e$\leanref{trace-semantics}.
Intuitively, $J_e(A\times B)$ is the probability mass of successfully
returning executions whose trace lies in $A$ and whose returned value
lies in $B$.

The two marginals of $J_e$ recover our quantities of interest. For
each measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$, its trace marginal
$\trlaw e(A)$\leanref{trace-law} is the probability mass of successful
executions whose trace lies in~$A$. In particular,
$\trlaw e(\{\tau\})$ is the probability that $e$ successfully terminates
with exactly the trace~$\tau$. Similarly, for each Borel set
$B\subseteq\Real$, its output marginal
$\law e(B)$\leanref{trace-erasure} is the probability mass of
successful executions whose returned value lies in $B$. This is
precisely the output measure defined in
\cref{fig:semantics}(\subref{fig:semantics-output}). Evaluating both
marginals on their full spaces gives the following:

\begin{theorem}[Equality of total masses\leanref{equality-of-total-masses}]
For every expression $e$,
\[
  \trlaw e(\Trace)
  =J_e(\Trace\times\Real)
  =\law e(\Real).
\]
\end{theorem}
Thus both marginals have the same total mass: the probability that $e$
successfully returns.

Let us first consider how determinization affects the trace marginal
$\trlaw e$. Determinization preserves both domain safety and this trace
marginal:

\begin{lemma}[Trace preservation\leanref{trace-preservation}]
\label{lem:trace-preservation}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. Then:
%
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\trans e$ is domain-safe, and
  \item $\trlaw{\trans e}=\trlaw e$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Conditioning on traces.}
The joint semantics not only recovers the trace and output marginals.
Conditioning the joint big-step measure $J_e$ on its trace component
gives the output distribution after a trace has been fixed. Since
$\Trace$ and $\Real$ are standard Borel spaces, the disintegration
theorem guarantees the existence of a conditional output distribution,
unique $\trlaw e$-almost everywhere. For measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$ and $B\subseteq\Real$, we denote this distribution by
$\law e(\cdot\mid\tau)$, satisfying
\begin{align}\label{eq:disintegration}
  J_e(A\times B)
  =\int_A\law e(B\mid\tau)\,\trlaw e(d\tau)~.
\end{align} 
Unlike conditioning by dividing by the probability of an individual trace,
this characterization remains valid even when individual traces have
probability zero. On a set of traces of $\trlaw e$-measure zero, the
conditional distribution is not uniquely determined, but its value
does not affect the integral above. We write
\begin{align*}
  \Expect[e\mid\tau]
  :=\int_{\Real}r^+\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)
  -\int_{\Real}r^-\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)
\end{align*}
for the expectation of $\law e(\,\cdot\mid\tau)$, which is the expected
return value of the original program after fixing a trace $\tau$ and
averaging over the remaining $\E$-mode randomness. We can now state
tracewise soundness.

\begin{theorem}[Tracewise soundness\leanref{trace-soundness}]
\label{thm:trace-soundness}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe.
For $\trlaw e$-almost every trace $\tau$,
\[
  \int_{\Real}|r|\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)<\infty
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \law{\trans e}(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  =\delta_{\Expect[e\mid\tau]}~.
\]
Moreover, for $\trlaw e$-almost every trace $\tau$,
\[
  \Expect[\trans e\mid\tau]=\Expect[e\mid\tau]~.
\]
\end{theorem}

This theorem statement does
not require the program to have a well-defined global expectation. For a concrete example, \Cref{sec:appendix-trace-soundness-example} illustrates how
tracewise soundness applies.
%Let us illustrate this theorem by means of :
%
%
%
Integrating tracewise soundness (\cref{thm:trace-soundness}) over the common
trace measure yields expectation preservation (\cref{thm:expectation}) when
the global expectation is well-defined, and variance non-increase
(\cref{thm:variance}) when the second moment is finite. More precisely, if
$q_e>0$ and $\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]$ is well-defined, the disintegration
identity (\cref{eq:disintegration}) gives
\[
  \Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
  =\frac{1}{q_e}
  \int_{\Trace}\Expect[e\mid\tau] \,\trlaw e(d\tau).\leanref{returned-expectation-along-traces}
\]
Note that such a normalization factor was not needed for the
conditional output distribution from \Cref{thm:trace-soundness}, since $\trlaw e$ records only traces
of successfully returning executions, so
$\law e(\Real\mid\tau)=1$ for $\trlaw e$-almost every trace $\tau$.
Trace preservation immediately implies that determinization preserves
the probability of successful return, meaning that the original and
transformed programs are conditioned on the same returning mass:
\[
  q_{\trans e}
  =\trlaw{\trans e}(\Trace)
  =\trlaw e(\Trace)
  =q_e.\leanref{output-mass}
\]

% Since determinization preserves the trace distribution and
% replaces conditional output distributions by point masses at their
% means with probability one, averaging over traces preserves
% expectation whenever the original program's global expectation is
% well-defined. This requirement is essential. Tracewise soundness
% guarantees finite conditional means outside a set of traces of measure
% zero, but this does not ensure that the original program has a
% well-defined global expectation. In the undefined-expectation example (\cref{ex:coin-flip}),
% every terminating trace has conditional mean zero, yet
% $\Expect[X^+]=\Expect[X^-]=\infty$, so the original program's global
% expectation is undefined.

Finally, we investigate how determinization
affects the probabilities of successful return, rejection, and
divergence. The results above concern the distribution of successfully
returned values, but determinization also preserves the probability of
returning at all. Let $d_e$ denote the probability of
divergence\leanref{divergence}, including rejection. Under domain safety,
executions cannot lose probability mass by getting stuck. Together
with preservation of output mass, this implies that determinization
also preserves the total probability of divergence and rejection.

\begin{proposition}[Probabilistic type safety\leanref{probabilistic-type-safety}]
\label{prop:probability}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. Then $\trans e$
is domain-safe, and
\[
  \law e(\Real)+d_e=1\leanref{return-or-diverge},
  \qquad
  \law{\trans e}(\Real)=\law e(\Real)\leanref{output-mass},
  \qquad
  d_{\trans e}=d_e.
\]
\end{proposition}
