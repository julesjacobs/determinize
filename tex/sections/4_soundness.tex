\section{Soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

Although determinization is a syntactic transformation, its semantic effect
can be characterized by conditioning on the random choices that it retains,
namely, the samples annotated with $\G$. Intuitively, once the outcomes of
these $\G$-mode samples are fixed, the remaining randomness comes only from
the $\E$-mode samples that determinization replaces by their means. Under
these fixed $\G$ choices, the transformed program returns the expected
output of the original program over the remaining $\E$-mode randomness.

This conditional characterization is the key to our soundness result and is useful for two reasons. First, it gives a local account of what determinization does even when the program's overall expectation is undefined. Second, when the global expectation does exist, expectation preservation follows by integrating over these fixed choices. Our results distinguish between finite, infinite, and undefined expectations, so let us first make this terminology precise.

\begin{definition}[Expectation\leanref{expectation-defined}]
Let $X$ be a real-valued random variable with probability distribution $\mu$, and set
\[
r^+=\max(r,0),
\qquad
r^-=\max(-r,0).
\]
Then
\[
\Expect[X^{+}]
=
\int_{\Real} r^{+}\,\mu(dr)
\in[0,\infty],
\qquad
\Expect[X^{-}]
=
\int_{\Real} r^{-}\,\mu(dr)
\in[0,\infty]
\]
and
\[
\Expect[X]
:=
\Expect[X^+]-\Expect[X^-].
\]
The expectation is \emph{well-defined in the extended reals} when the two terms are not both infinite; in this case it may be finite, $+\infty$, or $-\infty$. It is \emph{undefined} precisely when $\Expect[X^+]=\Expect[X^-]=\infty$, and is \emph{finite} exactly when
\[
\int_{\Real}|r|\,\mu(dr)<\infty.
\]
\end{definition}

The distinction is important even for simple programs, as the following example illustrates.

\begin{example}[Undefined Global Expectation]\label{ex:coin-flip}
Consider a program that flips a fair coin until tails and, after $n$ preceding heads, returns a sample from $\draw{uniform}\E{-2^n,2^n}$:

\begin{lstlisting}
let sample = rec f bound =>
  if bernoulli[G](1/2) < 1 then uniform[E](-bound, bound)
  else f (2 * bound)
in sample 1
\end{lstlisting}

For any fixed sequence of retained $\G$-samples, the remaining $\E$-sample has mean zero, so the determinized program returns zero. Globally, however, let $N$ be the number of heads and $X$ the returned value. We have $\Pr(N=n)=2^{-(n+1)}$. Conditioned on $N=n$,
$X\sim\kw{uniform}(-2^n,2^n)$ and hence
$\Expect[|X|\mid N=n]=2^{n-1}$.\footnote{First absolute moment. For
$U\sim\kw{uniform}(-a,a)$,
$\Expect[|U|]=(2a)^{-1}\int_{-a}^{a}|u|\,du=a/2$.} Therefore,
\[
  \Expect[|X|]
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Pr(N=n)\Expect[|X|\mid N=n]
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-(n+1)}2^{n-1}
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac14
  =\infty.
\]
Because each conditional distribution is symmetric, $\Expect[X^+]=\Expect[X^-]=\infty$, so the global expectation $\Expect[X]$ is undefined. This thus motivates stating our primary soundness result under fixed retained randomness before deriving global soundness when the relevant expectations exist.
\end{example}

In what follows, we first formalize this characterization using traces of the retained random choices, and then derive the global soundness. We state the main soundness theorems throughout this section, leaving the proof strategies to~\Cref{sec:proof} and the full proofs to~\Cref{sec:appendix}.

\subsection{Tracewise Soundness}
\label{sec:traces}

\input{figures/traces.tex}

We begin by making precise what it means to fix the random choices retained by
determinization.

\begin{definition}[$\G$-trace\leanref{traces}]
Let $\Trace$ be the space of finite sequences of $\G$-mode sampling outcomes. A \emph{$\G$-trace} is a sequence
\[
  \tau \in \Trace,
  \qquad
  \tau ::= [] \mid (D,r)::\tau,
\]
where each pair $(D,r)$ records the distribution primitive $D$ and sampled value $r$ of an executed $\G$-mode sampling operation along a successfully terminating execution.
\end{definition}

When the mode is clear, we simply call these \emph{traces}. Note that $\E$-mode samples do not appear in a trace: they remain random after the trace has been fixed. A trace may contain either discrete or continuous
values, and in the latter case an individual trace may itself have probability zero.

Now, we refine the output semantics to jointly record the trace and the
returned value, as shown in \cref{fig:traces}. Like the original output
measure $\law e$, this refined semantics records only executions that successfully return a real value and is therefore a subprobability measure; rejected
or diverging executions contribute no output pair. For each $n$, the measure $\joint e n$ records executions returning after exactly $n$ steps:
$\E$-mode samples are integrated out, whereas each $\G$-mode sample contributes
its distribution primitive $D$ and sampled value $r$ to the trace $\tau$.
Summing these measures over all $n$ steps gives the joint big-step
subprobability measure $J_e$\leanref{trace-semantics} on $\Trace\times\Real$. Intuitively, $J_e(A\times B)$ is the probability mass
of successfully returning executions whose trace lies in $A$ and whose
returned value lies in $B$.

The two marginals of $J_e$ recover our quantities of interest. For each measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$, its trace marginal $\trlaw e(A)$\leanref{trace-law} is the probability mass of successful executions whose trace lies in $A$. Similarly, for each measurable $B\subseteq\Real$, its output marginal $\law e(B)$\leanref{trace-erasure} is the probability mass of successful executions whose returned value lies in $B$. Evaluating both marginals on their full spaces gives
\[
  \trlaw e(\Trace)
  =
  J_e(\Trace\times\Real)
  =
  \law e(\Real).
\]
Thus both marginals have the same total mass: the probability that $e$
successfully returns.

We first consider how determinization affects the trace marginal $\trlaw e$. Recall a program is \emph{domain-safe}\leanref{domain-safety} if,
almost surely, every executed distribution has parameters in its domain and
every executed division has a nonzero divisor; this condition rules out loss of probability mass through invalid
operations. Determinization preserves both domain safety and this trace marginal:

\begin{lemma}[Trace preservation\leanref{trace-preservation}]
\label{lem:trace-preservation}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe.
\par
Then $\trans e$ is domain-safe and
\[
\trlaw{\trans e}=\trlaw e.
\]
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Conditioning on traces.} 
The joint semantics not only recovers the trace and output marginals. Conditioning the joint big-step measure \(J_e\) on its trace component gives the output distribution after a trace has been fixed. We write
\begin{align}\label{eq:disintegration}
  \law e(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  \quad\text{such that}\quad
  J_e(A\times B)=\int_A\law e(B\mid\tau)\,\trlaw e(d\tau)
\end{align}
for this conditional output distribution, for measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$ and $B\subseteq\Real$. This measure-theoretic formulation is necessary because a trace containing continuous $\G$-samples may itself have probability zero, so conditioning cannot in general be defined by dividing by the probability of an individual trace. We write 
\begin{align*}\label{eq:conditional-expectation}
  \Expect[e\mid\tau] :=
  \int_{\Real}r^+\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)
  -
  \int_{\Real}r^-\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)
\end{align*}
for the expectation of $\law e(\,\cdot\mid\tau)$, which is the expected return value of the original program after fixing a trace $\tau$ and averaging over the remaining $\E$-mode randomness. We can now state tracewise soundness.

\begin{theorem}[Tracewise soundness\leanref{trace-soundness}]
\label{thm:trace-soundness}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. For
$\trlaw e$-almost every trace $\tau$,
\[
  \law{\trans e}(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  =
  \delta_{\Expect[e\mid\tau]}.
\]
In particular,
\[
  \Expect[e\mid\tau],\ \Expect[\trans e\mid\tau]\in\Real,
  \qquad
  \Expect[\trans e\mid\tau]=\Expect[e\mid\tau].
\]
\end{theorem}

This statement does not require the program to have a global
expectation. It states that for all traces except a set of measure zero, the transformed program returns the average output of the original program under some fixed trace. When a global expectation is defined, however, \cref{thm:trace-soundness} can be integrated
over traces to obtain our global soundness guarantees, which we discuss next.

\subsection{Global Soundness}

We now recover the global notions of expectation and variance from the
tracewise characterization.

\paragraph{Conditioning on successful return.}
Recall that $\law e$ and $\trlaw e$ are subprobability measures, since diverging or rejected executions contribute no returned value. Thus, let
\[
q_e := \law e(\Real)=\trlaw e(\Trace)\leanref{return-probability}
\]
denote the probability that $e$ successfully returns. When $q_e>0$, we consider expectations and variances conditioned on successful return. In particular,
\begin{gather*}
\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
:=
\frac{1}{q_e}
\left(
  \int_{\Real} r^+\,\law e(dr)
  -
  \int_{\Real} r^-\,\law e(dr)
\right)
=
\frac{1}{q_e}
\int_{\Trace}
\Expect[e\mid\tau]\,\trlaw e(d\tau)\quad \leanref{returned-expectation},
\\
\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
:=
\frac{1}{q_e}
\int_{\Real}
\bigl(r-\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]\bigr)^2
\,\law e(dr)\quad\leanref{returned-variance}.
\end{gather*}

Note that such a normalization factor was not needed in~\Cref{sec:traces} since $\trlaw e$ records only traces of successfully returning executions, so $\law e(\Real\mid\tau) = 1$ for a fixed trace $\tau$. Trace preservation immediately implies that determinization preserves the probability of successful return, meaning that the original and transformed programs are conditioned on the same returning mass:
\[
q_{\trans e}
=
\trlaw{\trans e}(\Trace)
=
\trlaw e(\Trace)
=
q_e.
\leanref{output-mass}
\]

Now, since determinization preserves the trace distribution and replaces each conditional output distribution by its mean, averaging over traces preserves expectation. However, the requirement that the global expectation be well-defined is essential. Tracewise soundness guarantees a finite conditional mean for almost every fixed trace, but averaging these means over all traces need not yield a well-defined global expectation. Formally, we can state:

\begin{theorem}[Expectation preservation\leanref{expectation-preservation}]
\label{thm:expectation}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. If $q_e>0$ and
$\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]$ is well-defined in the extended reals, then $q_{\trans{e}} > 0$ and $\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]$ is also well-defined, and
\[
\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
=
\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e].
\]
\end{theorem}

Beyond preserving the global expectation, determinization also removes the variability due to $\E$-mode sampling within each trace, while retaining the variability induced by the $\G$-trace. It therefore cannot increase the variance of returned values.

\begin{theorem}[Variance non-increase\leanref{variance-non-increase}]
\label{thm:variance}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. If $q_e > 0$ and $\int_{\Real} r^2\,\law e(dr)<\infty$,
then $q_{\trans e} > 0$ and $\trans e$ has a finite second moment, and
\[
\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
\leq
\Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e].
\]
\end{theorem}

This variance reduction is a special case of a more general fact: determinization cannot increase the expected value of any nonnegative convex function of the program's return value. This follows by applying Jensen's inequality tracewise. In particular, when the second moment is finite, choosing
$\varphi(r)=(r-\Expect_{\mathrm{ret}}[e])^2$ and using expectation preservation yields the variance non-increase result above.

\begin{theorem}[Convex function inequality\leanref{convex-function-inequality}]
\label{thm:convex-function}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. Then, for every
nonnegative convex function $\varphi:\Real\to\Real$,
\[
  \int_{\Real}
    \varphi(r)\,\law{\trans e}(dr)
  \leq
  \int_{\Real}
    \varphi(r)\,\law e(dr),
\]
where both sides are understood as nonnegative extended integrals and may
therefore equal $+\infty$.
\end{theorem}

Finally, we end with the following point regarding how determinization affects the probabilities of successful return, rejection, and divergence. The results above concern the distribution of successfully returned values, but determinization also preserves the probability of returning at all. Let $d_e$ denote the probability of
divergence\leanref{divergence}, including rejection.  Under domain safety,
executions cannot lose probability mass by getting stuck. Together with preservation of output mass, this implies that determinization
also preserves the total probability of divergence and rejection.

\begin{proposition}[Probability preservation\leanref{probability-preservation}]
\label{prop:probability}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe. Then $\trans e$ is domain-safe,
and
\[
 \law e(\Real)+d_e=1\leanref{return-or-diverge},
 \qquad
 \law{\trans e}(\Real)=\law e(\Real)\leanref{output-mass}.
\]
\end{proposition}
