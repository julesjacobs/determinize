\begin{abstract}
Probabilistic programs often use continuous random variables, and their expected output is then estimated by sampling. In this paper we explore the insight that some of these random samples can be replaced by their means without changing the expected output, thereby reducing the variance of the estimate. We design \emph{determinization}, a type-directed, source-to-source transformation that automatically identifies and replaces such samples in higher-order, recursive probabilistic programs. Our type system tracks dependencies between random choices and restricts determinizable values to affine uses. This makes the replacements sound without symbolic marginalization. Using a symbolic operational semantics, we prove that determinization preserves expected values and never increases output variance, and mechanize these guarantees in Lean. Our evaluation shows that determinization reduces output variance on sampling benchmarks from prior work and on new examples, in some cases by more than two orders of magnitude. When the transformed program admits a finite Markov reward
representation supported by our exact backend, determinization also
enables exact expectation computation.
\end{abstract}
