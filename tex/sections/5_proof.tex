\section{Proof}
\label{sec:proof}

We now turn to the soundness of our determinization transformation. To motivate the proof strategy, first observe that at an $E$-mode distribution, the original program samples a value, whereas the transformed program computes the mean of the distribution. The resulting programs therefore have different output distributions. Thus, our proof relates both programs to a common symbolic execution, and the original and determiinzed programs are then obtained as two interpretations of the same symbolic execution. 

The symbolic semantics differs from the ordinary semantics only in its treatment of $\E$-mode draws. Rather than immediately sampling an $\E$-mode distribution or replacing it by its mean, symbolic execution records the draw using a fresh symbolic variable and continuous evaluating with that variable: wen symbolic execution encounters
$\draw D\E{\bar a}$
it does not sample from $D(\bar a)$. Instead, it introduces a fresh variable $u$, records the binding $u\sim D(\bar a)$ in a symbolic environment, and continues execution with $u$ in place of the sampled value. Dependencies between $\E$-mode draws are therefore preserved symbolically: the parameters of a later distribution may refer to variables introduced by earlier draws. By contrast, $\G$-mode draws are sampled immediately, exactly as in the ordinary semantics.

\paragraph{Interpretation of initialized symbolic semantics.}
Recall that
\[
  \mathsf{init}(e)
  =
  (\emptyset\mid\mathsf{lift}(e)),
\]
where $\mathsf{lift}$ embeds an ordinary expression into the symbolic
language. The following theorems show that the two interpretations of the
symbolic semantics recover the source and transformed output measures.

\begin{theorem}[Actual interpretation of initialized symbolic semantics]
\label{thm:actual-initialized-symbolic}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is closed. Then, for every $n\in\Nat$,
\[
  \mathsf{Act}
  \left(
    \widehat\mu_{\mathsf{init}(e)}^{(n)}
  \right)
  =
  \out e n.
\]
\end{theorem}

The actual interpretation samples the variables recorded in each terminal
symbolic state and substitutes the resulting valuation into its symbolic
output. Thus the theorem states that delaying the $\E$-mode samples
symbolically and sampling them at the end recovers exactly the source
program's output distribution at every termination depth.

\begin{theorem}[Expected interpretation of initialized symbolic semantics]
\label{thm:expected-initialized-symbolic}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$, $e$ is closed, and $e$ is domain-safe. Then, for
every $n\in\Nat$,
\[
  \mathsf{Exp}
  \left(
    \widehat\mu_{\mathsf{init}(e)}^{(n)}
  \right)
  =
  \out{\trans e}n.
\]
\end{theorem}

The expected interpretation replaces the variables recorded in each
symbolic environment by their sequential means. The theorem states that
performing this replacement after symbolic evaluation agrees exactly with
executing the determinized program.



\paragraph{Agreement of interpretations.}
The preceding theorems recover two different output distributions from the
same symbolic semantics. The key observation is that, after fixing the
$\G$-mode choices, these distributions have the same expectation.

\begin{theorem}[Agreement of interpretations]
\label{thm:agreement-of-interpretations}
Let $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ be closed and well typed, and let
$\tau\in\Trace$ be a valid $\G$ trace. Suppose symbolic evaluation of
$\mathsf{init}(e)$ along $\tau$ terminates in
\[
  s^\tau
  =
  (\sigma\mid a:\tyR\E),
\]
where
\[
  a=c_0+\sum_{i=1}^k c_i u_i
\]
is affine in the variables recorded by $\sigma$. If $\sigma$ is integrable,
then both interpretations have finite first absolute moments:
\[
  \int_{\Real}|r|\,\mathsf{Act}(s^\tau)(dr)<\infty
\]
and
\[
  \int_{\Real}|r|\,\mathsf{Exp}(s^\tau)(dr)<\infty.
\]
Moreover, their expectations agree:
\[
  \int_{\Real}r\,\mathsf{Act}(s^\tau)(dr)
  =
  \int_{\Real}r\,\mathsf{Exp}(s^\tau)(dr).
\]
\end{theorem}


\begin{theorem}[Generalized convex inequality]
Let $\varphi:\Real\to\overline{\Real}$ be measurable, convex, and
bounded below as required for the relevant integrals to exist. Then
\[
  \int_{\Real}
    \varphi(r)\,\law{\trans e}(dr)
  \le
  \int_{\Real}
    \varphi(r)\,\law e(dr).
\]
\end{theorem}
