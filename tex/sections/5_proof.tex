\section{Proof}
\label{sec:proof}

We now turn to the soundness of our determinization transformation. To motivate the proof strategy, first observe that at an $E$-mode distribution, the original program samples a value, whereas the transformed program computes the mean of the distribution. The resulting programs therefore have different output distributions. Thus, our proof relates both programs to a common symbolic execution, and the original and determiinzed programs are then obtained as two interpretations of the same symbolic execution. 

The symbolic semantics differs from the ordinary semantics only in its treatment of $\E$-mode draws Instead of immediately interpreting an $\E$-mode distribution as either a sample or a mean, symbolic execution records it using a fresh variable. The resulting
symbolic execution admits two interpretations: an \emph{actual}
interpretation that samples the recorded variables, recovering the source program, and an \emph{expected} interpretation that substitutes their sequential means, recovering the determinized program. Our proof thus proceeds in four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item define symbolic execution and its two interpretations;
  \item show that both interpretations commute with symbolic reduction;
  \item prove that the interpretations agree after conditioning on retained
        randomness;
  \item sum over termination depths and derive the tracewise and global
        soundness results.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Symbolic Execution}

A symbolic configuration has the form
\[
  s=(\sigma\mid\widehat e:\rho),
\]
where $\widehat e$ is a residual symbolic expression of type $\rho$ and
$\sigma$ is an ordered symbolic environment
\[
  \sigma
  ::=
  \emptyset
  \mid
  \sigma,\ u\sim D(\bar a).
\]
Each declaration records a fresh variable $u$ together with the distribution
from which it would be sampled. Its parameters $\bar a$ may be affine
expressions over variables declared earlier in $\sigma$. The order of these
declarations is significant, since a later distribution may depend on the
values of earlier symbolic variables.

\input{figures/symbolic-semantics.tex}

As shown in \cref{fig:symbolic-semantics}, ordinary deterministic reductions
proceed as in the concrete semantics. An $\M$-mode distribution with
symbolic arguments reduces to the corresponding symbolic mean. When
symbolic execution encounters an $\E$-mode distribution
\[
  \draw D\E{\bar a},
\]
it introduces a fresh variable $u$, appends the declaration
$u\sim D(\bar a)$ to $\sigma$, and continues with $u$ in place of the
distribution expression. This step neither samples a value nor computes a
mean. It only records the probabilistic dependency for later interpretation.

In contrast, a $\G$-mode distribution is sampled immediately, as in the
ordinary semantics. Its primitive and sampled value are recorded in the
transition label $(D,r)$ and therefore contribute to the retained trace.
All deterministic and $\E$-mode steps carry the silent label $\epsilon$.
Symbolic execution consequently delays exactly the randomness removed by
determinization while executing the retained randomness normally.

For example, symbolic execution of
\[
  \kw{let}\ x=\kw{uniform}^{\E}(0,2)\ \kw{in}\
  \kw{gaussian}^{\E}(x+1,1)
\]
produces an environment of the form
\[
  \sigma
  =
  \bigl(
    u_1\sim\kw{uniform}(0,2),\
    u_2\sim\kw{gaussian}(u_1+1,1)
  \bigr)
\]
and the residual expression $u_2$. The occurrence of $u_1$ in the second
declaration preserves the dependency between the two samples. The symbolic
configuration can subsequently be interpreted either by sampling the
recorded variables in order or by replacing them with their sequential
means. These two interpretations recover the source and determinized
programs, respectively.


\subsection{Two Interpretations of Symbolic Execution}
\label{sec:symbolic-interpretations}

A symbolic configuration records $\E$-mode samples without deciding whether
they should be sampled, as in the source program, or replaced by their
means, as in the transformed program. We recover these two behaviors through
the \emph{actual} and \emph{expected} interpretations.

A valuation $\theta$ assigns a real value to every symbolic variable declared
in $\sigma$. We write
\[
  \mathsf{realize}_\theta(\widehat e)
\]
for the ordinary expression obtained by substituting these values into the
symbolic residual expression $\widehat e$.

For a symbolic environment
\[
  \sigma
  =
  \bigl(
    u_1\sim D_1(\bar a_1),\ldots,
    u_k\sim D_k(\bar a_k)
  \bigr),
\]
let $\mathcal A_\sigma$ be the joint distribution over valuations obtained
by sampling the declarations in order. In particular, when sampling $u_i$,
the parameters $\bar a_i$ are evaluated using the previously sampled values
of $u_1,\ldots,u_{i-1}$. This sequential construction preserves dependencies
between recorded distributions.

The actual interpretation samples a valuation from $\mathcal A_\sigma$,
realizes the residual expression under that valuation, and then applies the
exact-depth trace semantics:
\[
  \mathsf{Act}_n(\sigma\mid\widehat e)
  =
  \int
    \joint{\mathsf{realize}_\theta(\widehat e)}n\,
    \mathcal A_\sigma(d\theta).
\]
Thus, $\mathsf{Act}_n$ interprets every recorded $\E$-mode distribution as an
ordinary sample, as in the source program.

The expected interpretation instead assigns each symbolic variable its
sequential mean. Let $\bar\theta_\sigma$ denote the valuation constructed in
declaration order by
\[
  \bar\theta_\sigma(u_i)
  =
  \operatorname{Mean}_{D_i}
  \bigl(\bar a_i[\bar\theta_\sigma]\bigr),
\]
where $\bar a_i[\bar\theta_\sigma]$ substitutes the means already assigned
to the preceding variables. We then realize the residual expression under
this valuation and determinize any $\E$-mode distributions that have not yet
been encountered by symbolic execution:
\[
  \mathsf{Exp}_n(\sigma\mid\widehat e)
  =
  \joint{
    \trans{
      \mathsf{realize}_{\bar\theta_\sigma}(\widehat e)
    }
  }n.
\]
Consequently, $\mathsf{Exp}_n$ interprets the recorded variables, as well as
any remaining $\E$ sites, according to the transformed program.

Both $\mathsf{Act}_n(s)$ and $\mathsf{Exp}_n(s)$ are measures on
$\Trace\times\Real$. They describe traces and returned values rather than
distributions over symbolic configurations. Their difference lies only in
how they interpret the delayed $\E$-mode randomness: the actual
interpretation samples it, while the expected interpretation replaces it by
its sequential means.

For example, consider the symbolic environment
\[
  \sigma
  =
  \bigl(
    u_1\sim\kw{uniform}(0,2),\
    u_2\sim\kw{gaussian}(u_1+1,1)
  \bigr).
\]
Under $\mathcal A_\sigma$, the actual interpretation first samples $u_1$ and
then samples $u_2$ from a Gaussian whose mean depends on that value. The
sequential mean valuation instead assigns
\[
  \bar\theta_\sigma(u_1)=1,
  \qquad
  \bar\theta_\sigma(u_2)=2.
\]
This illustrates why the means must be computed in declaration order rather
than independently.

To connect these interpretations to ordinary programs, define the initial
symbolic configuration by
\[
  \mathsf{init}(e)
  =
  \bigl(\emptyset\mid\mathsf{lift}(e)\bigr),
\]
where $\mathsf{lift}$ embeds an ordinary expression into the symbolic
language. Since the initial environment is empty, realizing the lifted
expression makes no substitutions.

\begin{lemma}[Realization of initialization]
For every ordinary expression $e$,
\[
  \mathsf{realize}_{()}
  \bigl(\mathsf{lift}(e)\bigr)
  =
  e.
\]
\end{lemma}

The next step is to show that both interpretations commute with symbolic
reduction. Iterating these commuting results will establish that the actual
interpretation of $\mathsf{init}(e)$ recovers the source semantics, while
the expected interpretation recovers the semantics of $\trans e$.

\subsection{Commuting Lemmas}
\label{sec:commuting-lemmas}

We first show that symbolic reduction is compatible with both
interpretations. For a silent symbolic step, interpreting the configuration
before the step at depth $n+1$ gives the same measure as interpreting the
resulting configuration at depth $n$. For a $\G$-mode sample, both
interpretations integrate over the same primitive distribution and record
the same event in the trace.

The actual interpretation must additionally account for delayed $\E$-mode
samples. Extending the symbolic environment postpones a sample without
changing its joint distribution, since $\mathcal A_\sigma$ eventually
samples the recorded declarations in the same order.

\begin{lemma}[Actual one-step commuting]
\label{lem:actual-one-step-commuting}
Let $s$ be a well-typed, domain-safe symbolic configuration.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item If
  \[
    s\xrightarrow{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{sym}}s',
  \]
  then, for every $n\in\Nat$,
  \[
    \mathsf{Act}_{n+1}(s)
    =
    \mathsf{Act}_n(s').
  \]

  \item Suppose
  \[
    s
    =
    (\sigma\mid
      \widehat C[\draw D\G{\bar r}]:\rho)
  \]
  and, for each $r\in\Real$, let
  \[
    s_r
    =
    (\sigma\mid\widehat C[r]:\rho).
  \]
  If $\operatorname{Domain}_D(\bar r)$, then
  \[
    \mathsf{Act}_{n+1}(s)
    =
    \int_{\Real}
      (p_{D,r})_*
      \mathsf{Act}_n(s_r)\,
      D(\bar r)(dr).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Here $p_{D,r}$ prefixes the event $(D,r)$ to the trace produced by the
continuation. The first part of the lemma handles deterministic reductions
and the recording of $\E$-mode samples, both of which produce silent
symbolic steps. The second part handles retained sampling explicitly.

The expected interpretation satisfies the analogous equations. In the
$\E$-mode case, extending the symbolic environment assigns the fresh
variable its sequential mean, which agrees with evaluating the corresponding
$\M$-mode distribution in the transformed program.

\begin{lemma}[Expected one-step commuting]
\label{lem:expected-one-step-commuting}
Let $s$ be a well-typed, domain-safe symbolic configuration.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item If
  \[
    s\xrightarrow{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{sym}}s',
  \]
  then, for every $n\in\Nat$,
  \[
    \mathsf{Exp}_{n+1}(s)
    =
    \mathsf{Exp}_n(s').
  \]

  \item Suppose
  \[
    s
    =
    (\sigma\mid
      \widehat C[\draw D\G{\bar r}]:\rho)
  \]
  and
  \[
    s_r
    =
    (\sigma\mid\widehat C[r]:\rho).
  \]
  If $\operatorname{Domain}_D(\bar r)$, then
  \[
    \mathsf{Exp}_{n+1}(s)
    =
    \int_{\Real}
      (p_{D,r})_*
      \mathsf{Exp}_n(s_r)\,
      D(\bar r)(dr).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Iterating these commuting lemmas connects symbolic execution to the ordinary
semantics. Starting from an empty symbolic environment, the actual
interpretation recovers the source program, while the expected
interpretation recovers its determinization.

\begin{theorem}[Actual interpretation of initialized symbolic execution]
\label{thm:actual-initialized-symbolic}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is closed. Then, for every
$n\in\Nat$,
\[
  \mathsf{Act}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  =
  \joint e n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Expected interpretation of initialized symbolic execution]
\label{thm:expected-initialized-symbolic}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is closed. Then, for every
$n\in\Nat$,
\[
  \mathsf{Exp}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  =
  \joint{\trans e}n.
\]
\end{theorem}

These results establish that the two interpretations describe the programs
we intend to compare. It remains to relate the two measures obtained from
the common symbolic execution.

\subsection{Agreement of the Two Interpretations}
\label{sec:interpretation-agreement}

The central step of the proof compares the actual and expected
interpretations of an arbitrary well-typed symbolic configuration. The
actual interpretation retains the full distribution of the delayed
variables, whereas the expected interpretation substitutes their sequential
means. Nevertheless, they induce the same distribution over retained
$\G$-randomness.

The proof proceeds over symbolic execution at a fixed depth. Deterministic
steps are handled by the commuting lemmas. At a $\G$-mode sample, both
interpretations integrate over the same distribution and extend the trace
in the same way. At an $\E$-mode sample, the actual interpretation extends
the joint distribution of symbolic variables, while the expected
interpretation extends the sequential mean valuation. The typing
restrictions ensure that the relevant dependence on these variables is
affine, so integration under the actual interpretation agrees with
evaluation at the sequential means under the expected interpretation.
Domain safety ensures that every distribution and primitive mean used in
this argument is well defined.

\begin{theorem}[Agreement of interpretations]
\label{thm:agreement}
Let $s$ be a well-typed, integrable, domain-safe symbolic configuration.
Then, for every $n\in\Nat$, $\mathsf{Act}_n(s)$ and
$\mathsf{Exp}_n(s)$ have the same trace marginal: for every measurable
$A\subseteq\Trace$,
\[
  \mathsf{Act}_n(s)(A\times\Real)
  =
  \mathsf{Exp}_n(s)(A\times\Real).
\]
Moreover, for almost every trace $\tau$ under this common marginal,
\[
  \int_{\Real}
    |r|\,\mathsf{Act}_n(s)(dr\mid\tau)
  <\infty
\]
and
\[
  \mathsf{Exp}_n(s)(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  =
  \delta_{
    \displaystyle
    \int_{\Real}
      r\,\mathsf{Act}_n(s)(dr\mid\tau)
  }.
\]
\end{theorem}

The theorem has two conclusions. First, the two interpretations have the
same trace marginal, so replacing delayed samples by their means does not
change the retained randomness of successfully returning executions.
Second, after conditioning on that retained randomness, the expected
interpretation is concentrated at the conditional mean of the actual
interpretation. This is the key property from which trace soundness follows.

\subsection{From Exact-Depth Agreement to Trace Soundness}
\label{sec:exact-depth-to-traces}

We now apply the agreement theorem to the initial symbolic configuration
$\mathsf{init}(e)$. The two interpretation theorems identify its actual and
expected interpretations with the exact-depth joint semantics of $e$ and
$\trans e$, respectively. We therefore obtain agreement at each finite
termination depth.

\begin{theorem}[Initialized exact-depth tracewise agreement]
\label{thm:initialized-exact-depth-agreement}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is closed and domain-safe. Then, for every
$n\in\Nat$, $\joint e n$ and $\joint{\trans e}n$ have the same trace
marginal: for every measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$,
\[
  \joint e n(A\times\Real)
  =
  \joint{\trans e}n(A\times\Real).
\]
Moreover, for almost every trace $\tau$ under this common marginal,
\[
  \int_{\Real}|r|\,\joint e n(dr\mid\tau)<\infty
\]
and
\[
  \joint{\trans e}n(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  =
  \delta_{
    \displaystyle
    \int_{\Real}r\,\joint e n(dr\mid\tau)
  }.
\]
\end{theorem}

The exact-depth measures account only for executions returning after a fixed
number of steps. Summing over all depths recovers the complete joint
trace--output measures.

\begin{corollary}[Big-step interpretation of initialization]
Under the preceding assumptions,
\[
  \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
    \mathsf{Act}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  =
  J_e
\]
and
\[
  \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
    \mathsf{Exp}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  =
  J_{\trans e}.
\]
Consequently, for every measurable $B\subseteq\Real$,
\[
  \left(
    \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
      \mathsf{Act}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  \right)(\Trace\times B)
  =
  \law e(B)
\]
and
\[
  \left(
    \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
      \mathsf{Exp}_n\bigl(\mathsf{init}(e)\bigr)
  \right)(\Trace\times B)
  =
  \law{\trans e}(B).
\]
\end{corollary}

It follows that the complete source and transformed joint measures have the
same trace marginal:
\[
  \trlaw{\trans e}=\trlaw e.
\]
Disintegrating the two joint measures with respect to this common marginal
then gives, for $\trlaw e$-almost every trace $\tau$,
\[
  \law{\trans e}(\,\cdot\mid\tau)
  =
  \delta_{\Expect[e\mid\tau]}.
\]
The preservation invariants of symbolic execution also show that
$\trans e$ is domain-safe whenever $e$ is. Together, these conclusions prove
\cref{thm:trace-soundness}.

\subsection{Deriving the Global Consequences}
\label{sec:proof-global-consequences}

The global results follow by marginalizing and integrating the tracewise
characterization. Because the trace and output marginals of a joint measure
have the same total mass, equality of the trace measures immediately gives
\[
  \law{\trans e}(\Real)=\law e(\Real).
\]
Thus determinization preserves the probability of successfully returning.

\paragraph{Expectation preservation.}
When the global expectation is defined, the law of total expectation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \int_{\Real}r\,\law{\trans e}(dr)
  &=
  \int_{\Trace}
    \Expect[e\mid\tau]\,
    \trlaw e(d\tau)\\
  &=
  \int_{\Real}r\,\law e(dr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The same return probability normalizes both output measures, proving
\cref{thm:expectation}. For extended expectations, the argument is applied
separately to the positive and negative parts of the output. The assumption
that the source expectation is defined excludes the case in which both
parts are infinite.

\paragraph{Convex functions.}
Let $\varphi:\Real\to\Real$ be nonnegative and convex. Trace soundness and
Jensen's inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \int_{\Real}\varphi(r)\,\law{\trans e}(dr)
  &=
  \int_{\Trace}
    \varphi\bigl(\Expect[e\mid\tau]\bigr)\,
    \trlaw e(d\tau)\\
  &\leq
  \int_{\Trace}
    \left(
      \int_{\Real}
        \varphi(r)\,\law e(dr\mid\tau)
    \right)
    \trlaw e(d\tau)\\
  &=
  \int_{\Real}\varphi(r)\,\law e(dr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The inequality is Jensen's inequality for each conditional output law, and
the final equality follows from disintegration and Tonelli's theorem. This
proves \cref{thm:convex-function}.

\paragraph{Variance.}
Assume $q_e=\law e(\Real)>0$ and that the source has a finite second moment.
Applying the law of total variance to the output conditioned on returning
gives
\[
  \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e]
  =
  \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
  +
  \Expect_{\tau\sim\trlaw e/q_e}
  \left[
    \Var\bigl(\law e(\,\cdot\mid\tau)\bigr)
  \right].
\]
The second term is nonnegative and measures exactly the variation remaining
in the source after its retained randomness has been fixed. This proves the
trace variance decomposition and, immediately,
\[
  \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[\trans e]
  \leq
  \Var_{\mathrm{ret}}[e].
\]

\paragraph{Divergence and rejection.}
Finally, domain safety rules out missing mass caused by invalid primitive
operations. Every execution therefore either successfully returns or
continues forever, where rejection is included as divergence:
\[
  \law e(\Real)+d_e=1.
\]
Since determinization preserves output mass, it also preserves the combined
probability of divergence and rejection, completing the proof of
\cref{prop:probability}.
