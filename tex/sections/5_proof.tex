\section{Symbolic Semantics}
\label{sec:proof}
\label{sec:symbolic-semantics}

This section outlines the proof strategy behind our soundness theorems.
Since determinization changes the output distribution, its correctness
cannot be established by equating the returned values of individual source and
transformed executions. Instead, we introduce a \emph{symbolic operational semantics} that
represents both programs in a common computation, postponing the random choices
that determinization eliminates. We develop the construction using the
signal-and-measurement program from the introduction. Here, determinization retains the uniform draw but replaces the two Gaussian draws by their means. The proof explains why these replacements preserve the conditional expected output, even though the Gaussian draws are dependent.

\paragraph{A common symbolic execution.}
Rather than sampling an $\E$-mode distribution immediately, symbolic execution
records it and continues with a variable standing for its result. This requires
a residual expression whose real literals may be \emph{affine expressions} in
previously recorded variables. Formally, we use the following states.

\begin{definition}[Symbolic state]
A symbolic state $s\in\mathsf{SymState}$ has the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
 s&::=(\sigma\mid\widehat e),\qquad
           \widehat e\in\widehat{\mathrm{Expr}},\\
 \sigma&::=\emptyset\mid\sigma,\ u\sim D(\bar a),\\
 a&=c_0+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_i u_i,\qquad c_0,\ldots,c_k\in\Real.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here, $\widehat e$ is a residual symbolic expression\leanref{symbolic-expression},
$a$ is an affine expression\leanref{symbolic-affine}, and $\sigma$ is an
ordered symbolic environment\leanref{symbolic-environment}. Each declaration
$u\sim D(\bar a)$ introduces a fresh variable whose distribution parameters
may refer only to earlier variables in $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\input{figures/symbolic-example.tex}

\Cref{fig:symbolic-example} follows our running program. It begins in
$\mathsf{init}(e)=(\emptyset\mid\mathsf{lift}(e))$, where $\mathsf{lift}$\leanref{symbolic-lifting}
embeds each real literal $r$ as the constant symbolic literal $\sym r$.
The $\G$-mode uniform draw is sampled as usual; suppose it produces $x=t$.
This draw contributes $\tau_t=[(\kw{uniform},t)]$ to the retained trace,
but is not added to $\sigma$. The first $\E$-mode Gaussian instead introduces
$u\sim\mathcal N(t,1)$, and the second introduces
$v\sim\mathcal N(u,1)$. After ordinary reductions, the final state is
\[
 s_t=(\sigma_t\mid\sym{tv}),\qquad
 \sigma_t=(u\sim\mathcal N(t,1),\ v\sim\mathcal N(u,1)).
\]
The residual expression is affine in $v$: the potentially nonlinear dependence
on $x$ is harmless because $t$ has already been fixed by a retained draw.
The environment preserves the dependence of $v$ on $u$ without choosing either
value. Its declarations induce the joint distribution
\[
 \mathcal A_{\sigma_t}(du,dv)
   =\mathcal N(t,1)(du)\,\mathcal N(u,1)(dv).
\]
More generally, for
$\sigma=(u_i\sim D_i(\bar a_i))_{i=1}^k$, the distribution
$\mathcal A_\sigma$\leanref{symbolic-actual-environment} samples
$\rho(u_i)\sim D_i(\bar a_i[\rho])$ in declaration order.

The symbolic reduction rules are given in \cref{fig:symbolic-semantics}.
Ordinary computation proceeds on affine expressions, including computing
the affine means of $\M$-mode primitives ($\textsc{Reduce}$), while an
$\E$-mode draw extends $\sigma$
($\textsc{Sample-E}$). Both are deterministic symbolic actions
$s\deterministicarrow_{\mathrm{sym}}s'$. A $\G$-mode draw instead makes a
sampling action $s\samplingarrow{\mu}_{\mathrm{sym}}k$, where $\mu$ is the
distribution of the draw and $k:\Real\to\mathsf{SymState}$ maps each sampled
value to its successor state. Thus, only retained choices are sampled during
symbolic execution. The full rules and measurability details appear in
\cref{sec:appendix-symbolic-rules}.

\subsection{Two Interpretations of the Symbolic Semantics}
\label{sec:symbolic-interpretations}

The significance of a symbolic state is that it supports two interpretations
of the \emph{same} deferred choices. In the \emph{actual interpretation}, we
sample a valuation $\rho$ from $\mathcal A_\sigma$ and substitute it into the
residual expression, recovering the source program's behavior. In the
\emph{expected interpretation}, we substitute the means of those choices and
determinize the remaining expression. Since later distributions can depend on
earlier samples, these means must be computed sequentially. For
$\sigma=(u_i\sim D_i(\bar a_i))_{i=1}^k$, define the
\emph{sequential mean valuation}\leanref{symbolic-mean-environment} by
\[
 \bar\rho_\sigma(u_i)
    :=\operatorname{Mean}_{D_i}(\bar a_i[\bar\rho_\sigma]),
       \qquad i=1,\ldots,k.
\]
In the running example this gives
$\bar\rho_{\sigma_t}(u)=t$ and
$\bar\rho_{\sigma_t}(v)=t$, whereas the actual valuation samples
$u\sim\mathcal N(t,1)$ followed by $v\sim\mathcal N(u,1)$.

To state the interpretations formally, realization
$\mathsf{realize}_\rho:\widehat{\mathrm{Expr}}\to\mathrm{Expr}$\leanref{symbolic-realization}
substitutes a valuation into symbolic literals:
\[
 \mathsf{realize}_\rho
    \bigl(\sym{c_0+\textstyle\sum_i c_i u_i}\bigr)
       =c_0+\sum_i c_i\rho(u_i).
\]
Lifting and realization otherwise act structurally; in particular,
$\mathsf{realize}_{()}(\mathsf{lift}(e))=e$\leanref{symbolic-realize-lift}.
As with $\joint e n$, we index both interpretations by the number of
remaining reduction steps.

\begin{definition}[Actual and expected interpretations]
For a symbolic state $s=(\sigma\mid\widehat e)$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Act}_n(s)
 &:=\int \joint{\mathsf{realize}_\rho(\widehat e)}n\,
                \mathcal A_\sigma(d\rho),\\
 \mathsf{Exp}_n(s)
 &:=\joint{\trans{\mathsf{realize}_{\bar\rho_\sigma}(\widehat e)}}n.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

These are measures on $\Trace\times\Real$. At initialization the environment
is empty, so the interpretations recover the two programs:
$\mathsf{Act}_n(\mathsf{init}(e))=\joint e n$ and
$\mathsf{Exp}_n(\mathsf{init}(e))=\joint{\trans e}n$.
At the terminal state $s_t$, no steps remain and no further $\G$-mode draws
occur. Because $v$ has marginal distribution $\mathcal N(t,2)$,
\cref{fig:symbolic-example} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Act}_0(s_t)(\{[]\}\times B)
   &=\mathcal N(t^2,2t^2)(B),\\
 \mathsf{Exp}_0(s_t)(\{[]\}\times B)
   &=\delta_{t^2}(B).
\end{aligned}
\]
The actual interpretation restores the deferred Gaussian noise, while the
expected interpretation returns its mean. This relationship is not peculiar
to Gaussians: for any well-typed, domain-safe symbolic environment and every
affine expression $a$,
\[
 \int a[\rho]\,\mathcal A_\sigma(d\rho)
       =a[\bar\rho_\sigma].
\]
It holds even for dependent declarations, and is precisely the property that
the typing restrictions preserve through symbolic evaluation.

\subsection{Deriving the Soundness Results}

\paragraph{Finite-step factorization.}
The two interpretations need not have the same output distribution. To relate
them, we introduce a trace-indexed kernel $K_{n,s}(\tau,\cdot)$ that restores
the source's conditional output distribution after $n$ remaining steps:
$\G$-mode outcomes are fixed by $\tau$, while $\E$-mode choices are integrated
out. Its construction is given in the appendix. The affine-mean invariant,
together with the correspondence of the two interpretations under each
symbolic reduction, yields the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-step factorization\leanref{finite-step-factorization}]
\label{thm:finite-step-factorization}
Let $s=(\sigma\mid\widehat e)$ be a well-typed,
domain-safe symbolic state with $\widehat e:\tyR\E$.
For every $n\in\Nat$ and measurable
$A\subseteq\Trace$, $B\subseteq\Real$,
\[
 \mathsf{Act}_n(s)(A\times B)
   =\int_{A\times\Real}K_{n,s}(\tau,B)\,
                        \mathsf{Exp}_n(s)(d\tau,dr).
\]
Moreover, for $\mathsf{Exp}_n(s)$-almost every $(\tau,r)$,
$K_{n,s}(\tau,\cdot)$ is a probability measure with finite first absolute
moment, and
\[
 r=\int_{\Real}x\,K_{n,s}(\tau,dx).
\]
\end{theorem}

The first assertion reconstructs the actual interpretation by restoring the
uncertainty removed by the expected interpretation; the second says that its
mean is exactly the expected output. For our terminal state, this is visible
without any machinery:
\[
 K_{0,s_t}([],\cdot)=\mathcal N(t^2,2t^2),
 \qquad \int_{\Real}r\,K_{0,s_t}([],dr)=t^2.
\]
The theorem establishes this relationship for every well-typed, domain-safe
symbolic state and every number of remaining steps, rather than only for a
finished execution of the example.

\paragraph{From finite steps to whole programs.}
To obtain a statement about complete executions, we combine the finite-step
kernels $K_{n,\mathsf{init}(e)}$ across all possible return times into a
whole-program kernel $K_e$. A compatibility lemma ensures that the resulting
factorization is preserved when we sum over step counts; the construction and
proof are deferred to the appendix.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-program factorization\leanref{whole-program-factorization}]
\label{thm:whole-program-factorization}
Suppose $\vdash e:\tyR\E$ and $e$ is domain-safe.
For all measurable $A\subseteq\Trace$ and $B\subseteq\Real$,
\[
 J_e(A\times B)
   =\int_{A\times\Real}K_e(\tau,B)\,
                         J_{\trans e}(d\tau,dr).
\]
Moreover, for $J_{\trans e}$-almost every $(\tau,r)$,
$K_e(\tau,\cdot)$ is a probability measure with finite first absolute
moment, and
\[
 r=\int_{\Real}x\,K_e(\tau,dx).
\]
\end{theorem}

The kernel is normalized on successful traces, so the factorization first
implies that the source and determinized programs have the same trace measure
$\eta$. Relative to this common measure, $K_e(\tau,\cdot)$ is the source's
conditional output distribution $\law e(\cdot\mid\tau)$, with mean
$m(\tau):=\int x\,K_e(\tau,dx)=\Expect[e\mid\tau]$.
The second part of the theorem says that the determinized output is almost
surely equal to $m(\tau)$ once the retained trace has been fixed. Consequently,
\[
 \law{\trans e}(\cdot\mid\tau)
   =\delta_{m(\tau)}
   =\delta_{\Expect[e\mid\tau]},
\]
which is the tracewise soundness guarantee of \cref{thm:trace-soundness}.
For the running example, the trace $\tau_t$ fixes $x=t$, giving
\[
 \law e(\cdot\mid\tau_t)=\mathcal N(t^2,2t^2),
 \qquad
 \law{\trans e}(\cdot\mid\tau_t)=\delta_{t^2}.
\]
Averaging over the shared trace measure then yields expectation preservation
whenever the original expectation is well-defined; the law of total variance
gives variance non-increase when the second moment is finite. In particular,
for this example both expected outputs equal
$\int_0^1 t^2\,dt=1/3$, while the variance drops from $34/45$ to $4/45$.
The full measure-theoretic derivation is given in
\cref{sec:appendix-trace-soundness}.
